Prayer Wheel

From hand-turned drums to nano film, and a wheel that cycles prayers entirely in memory.

Welcome. We built a digital prayer wheel for this hackathon. First, a quick tour of where prayer wheels come from and how makers have tried to put more prayers in every turn, then a look at ours.

History

What is a prayer wheel?

A drum of mantras

Mantras are written or printed on scrolls and wound inside a hollow cylinder on a spindle.

Turned, not recited

In Tibetan Buddhist practice, each turn is understood to send out every mantra inside.

The Dharma wheel

It makes physical the phrase "turning the wheel of Dharma", which describes how the Buddha taught.

02 · History

A prayer wheel is a cylinder holding mantras. In the Tibetan Buddhist tradition, turning it is understood to carry the benefit of every mantra inside. The image is tied to the idea of turning the wheel of Dharma.

History

Where it began

Tradition

Tibetan lineages trace the wheel to the Indian master Nagarjuna, who is said to have brought it to India from a naga king.

c. 400 CE

A Chinese pilgrim is reported to have written of the earliest wind-driven wheels, in Ladakh.

618–640

Mahayana Buddhism reaches Tibet from India under King Songtsen Gampo, and the practice takes root.

Lineage

Masters including Tilopa, Naropa, Marpa and Milarepa are said to have taught it.

03 · History · Dates and origin stories vary by source

The origin story is traditional, so treat the dates as approximate. Tibetan teachings trace the wheel to Nagarjuna. The earliest written reference is usually dated to around 400 CE, to a Chinese pilgrim in Ladakh. Buddhism came to Tibet in the reign of Songtsen Gampo. Source: Wikipedia, Prayer wheel; Lama Yeshe Wisdom Archive.

The mantra

Om Mani Padme Hum

The mantra of Avalokiteshvara, the bodhisattva of compassion. It decorates prayer wheels in ornamental Lantsa script.

04 · History

Om Mani Padme Hum is the mantra most often found inside and on prayer wheels. It belongs to Avalokiteshvara, the bodhisattva of compassion.

History

Many ways to turn

Hand

Held and turned by a person.

Water

A stream turns the drum.

Wind

Moving air spins it.

Heat

Rising warmth from a flame.

Electric

A motor does the turning.

The earliest recorded wheels were wind-driven. Each new source of power frees the turner and keeps the wheel going.

05 · History

Wheels have been turned by hand, water, wind, heat and electricity. Electric wheels were being built with microfilm inside them by the early 2000s, which leads into the next section.

Modern prayer wheels

What counts is how many mantras the wheel carries.

So makers asked: how many can we fit inside?

06 · Modern technology

Part of the tradition holds that the benefit of a turn scales with the number of mantras inside. A paper scroll is limited by volume. Modern makers use denser media: microfilm, then discs, then nano film.

Modern technology · Microfilm

Mantras on microfilm

18 billion

copies of one mantra on film, offered with every turn

Around 1993 the Dalai Lama encouraged using microfilm and bearings in prayer wheels.

Dorje Chang Institute in New Zealand miniaturized a mantra written by the Dalai Lama.

The film sits in an eight-foot wheel, with a goal of 2.5 trillion copies.

07 · Microfilm is a roll; microfiche is the same idea as flat sheets

Reports describe microfilm, a roll of film. Microfiche is the same idea as flat sheets, and both shrink text photographically so a tiny volume holds a huge number of copies. The 18 billion and 2.5 trillion figures are as reported by Tricycle magazine in 2004 about the Dorje Chang Institute in New Zealand. The 1993 suggestion from the Dalai Lama is as reported by Buddha Weekly and Vajrapani Institute.

Modern technology · Digital discs

Mantras on DVDs

128 DVDs

loaded with trillions of traditional mantras

Sakya Monastery of Seattle put mantras and prayers onto discs.

The discs went into a pair of one-foot copper wheels on Whidbey Island.

Digital storage turned the wheel into a small data carrier.

Modern technology · Nanotech

Mantras on nano film

20 billion

Mani mantras in a medium handheld wheel, as reported by makers

Nano-printed film fits many billions of mantras into a handheld wheel.

Single rolls are advertised at 33 billion mantras each.

These counts come from wheel makers, not independent measurement.

09 · Modern technology · Vendor claims

Be clear on this slide that the 20 billion and 33 billion figures are vendor claims, from Holy Land Prayer Wheels product pages. We could not find independent verification. The point is the trend: each new medium packs more mantras into a smaller wheel.

Modern technology

Smaller wheels, bigger counts

Paper

The traditional scroll, limited by its volume.

Microfilm

18 billion in an eight-foot wheel.

DVDs

Trillions in two one-foot wheels.

Nano film

About 20 billion in a handheld wheel.

Each step stores more prayers in less space. Our wheel takes the next step: no physical medium at all.

What if the medium were memory?

Prayers cycle in your browser's RAM. Nothing is printed, burned or stored.

11 · Our wheel

Now our project. Instead of a denser physical medium, we use volatile memory. This is the live demo moment if you want to show the wheel.

Our wheel

How it works

1 · SERVER

A shared catalog

The server hands every visitor the same small, replaceable list of prayers.

2 · BROWSER

A worker in memory

A background worker loops through the catalog, repeating, for about five seconds.

3 · AT REST

A count and a prayer

The wheel slows, then shows how many prayers passed through and one to keep.

12 · Our wheel

The server (PHP) serves a hard-coded catalog that can later be replaced by a shared list. The browser hands it to a Web Worker, which cycles it as many times as it can until the page tells it to stop. The page then shows the count and a prayer.

Demo here: spin the wheel, click again to accelerate it, and watch it slow and stop. The 181.8 million count comes from one real spin on the demo machine; on other devices the count will differ because it is however many fit in five seconds.

Our wheel

Nothing is stored

No disk wear

Prayers cycle in RAM, so nothing is written to a storage device.

No tracking

No database, browser storage, logs or analytics record a spin.

Gone on reload

Like a turning wheel, a spin leaves no trace once it stops.

14 · Our wheel

This is a design promise, and the code checks for it: we searched the app for browser storage, cookies, network calls and logging and found none.
